    This Trust Domain enables data exchange mediated by the pilgrim, on the basis of the pilgrim’s consent. The consents are recorded in the IPS.HAJJ.CONSENT CodeSystem, and each pilgrimage consent in a pilgrim’s IPS names one of them in Consent.policyRule.

    Terms of use

    The IPS.HAJJ.CONSENT ValueSet is this Trust Domain’s terms of use: the policies a participant may say it acts under. It is recorded on the IPS-PILGRIMAGE Trust Domain in the GDHCN Trust IG. The GDHCN Administrative and Operational Framework 1.2 has no annex for this Trust Domain yet, so that record is drafted from this IG.

    Explicit and implied consent

    This guide follows the consent model of the GDHCN Trust Network: Personal Health Wallet guide, which is built on IHE Privacy Consent on FHIR (PCF) 1.1.0:

    • Explicit consent. The pilgrim agrees by their own act: on their wallet, or to the counsellor, who records it. Each pilgrimage consent is an explicit consent, an opt-in: its Consent.category includes the HL7 v3 ActConsentDirective code OPTIN. It names its IPS.HAJJ.CONSENT code and the code system version in Consent.policyRule, and its source[x] is the evidence: the consent text the pilgrim was shown, with their recorded act. The Personal Health Wallet guide’s process L (“Record Explicit Consent”) describes the steps. Both the verbal consent and the consent on the wallet in this guide’s business processes are explicit consents.
    • Implied consent. The pilgrim does nothing, and the Trust Domain’s default policy applies (HL7 ActConsentDirective IMPLIED). This Trust Domain is opt-in: its default is the IHE PCF implicit policy Policy-deny. With no pilgrimage consent, the IPS is not shared.

    Consent directives travel with the document. The pilgrim’s IPS (HajjIPS) carries the pilgrimage consents it is shared under, as consent entries, and lists them in its Advance Directives section. The Personal Health Wallet guide’s process M (“Add Consent Directives to a Shared or Issued Document”) describes the steps. The copy in the IPS is evidence for the receiver. The origin HIE enforces the copy in its own Consent Registry, because a signed IPS cannot show a later withdrawal.

    Naming the consent a VHL Receiver acts under

    Under the Verifiable Credential Option of IHE ITI VHL (ITI-YY5), a VHL Receiver retrieves the manifest with a credential it issues itself. It can name the consent policy it acts under in that credential’s W3C VC 2.0 termsOfUse, with one Trust Domain policy entry per policy:

    {
  "@context": ["https://www.w3.org/ns/credentials/v2"],
  "type": ["VerifiableCredential", "VHLManifestCredential"],
  "issuer": "did:web:vhl-receiver.example.org",
  "validFrom": "2026-06-01T10:00:00Z",
  "validUntil": "2026-06-01T10:05:00Z",
  "credentialSubject": {
    "id": "https://vhl-sharer.example.org/List?_id=abc123def456",
    "manifest": { "url": "https://vhl-sharer.example.org/List?_id=abc123def456", "flag": "LP" },
    "recipient": "Ministry of Health, Taifafeki (fictional): pilgrimage facility"
  },
  "termsOfUse": [{
    "type": "http://smart.who.int/trust/StructureDefinition/TrustDomainPolicy",
    "id": "http://smart.who.int/ips-pilgrimage/CodeSystem/IPS.HAJJ.CONSENT#CONSENT-XXR"
  }],
  "proof": { "type": "DataIntegrityProof", "cryptosuite": "ecdsa-rdfc-2019", "proofPurpose": "assertionMethod", "verificationMethod": "did:web:vhl-receiver.example.org#key-1", "proofValue": "z…" }
}

    The VHL Sharer then:

    1. checks that each named policy is in this ValueSet;
    2. matches it against the Consent.policyRule of the pilgrim’s pilgrimage consents (system and code);
    3. decides as the GDHCN Trust Network: Personal Health Wallet process I describes.

    Naming a policy only narrows the consents that apply, and it never overrides a prohibition. A credential with no termsOfUse is decided on all the pilgrim’s applicable consents.
